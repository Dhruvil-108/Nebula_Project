1. Common System Architecture

React frontend → TanStack Query state/cache → Axios authenticated request → Express authentication and permission middleware → organizationId tenant scoping → controller and service business logic → Mongoose → MongoDB → response and UI refresh
  • Authentication: JWT identifies the current user and organization.
  • Multi-tenancy: Queries are scoped by organizationId, preventing cross-company data access.
  • RBAC: Actions such as view, create, edit, delete and approve are permission controlled.
  • Auditability: Important transitions create immutable or chronological activity records.

2. Recruitment / ATS Module

The Recruitment module manages the complete journey from identifying a vacancy to converting a selected candidate into an HRMS employee.

Manpower requirement → job requisition/opening → AI advertisement → candidate application → screening pipeline → interviews and scorecards → offer approval → acceptance → one-click HRMS onboarding

Step-by-step flow

  1. Create job opening: HR or a hiring manager enters title, department, employment type, location, experience, salary band, skills, recruiters, hiring manager and open positions. The normal status flow is draft → published → on_hold → closed.
  2. Generate advertisement: Gemini generates a job post in dynamic, technical or corporate tone. A deterministic template fallback keeps the feature usable if AI is unavailable.
  3. Receive candidate: Candidate profile stores contact details, resume, experience, compensation expectation, notice period, LinkedIn/GitHub/portfolio and source. A code such as CAND-2026-0012 is generated.
  4. Manage pipeline: Recruiters use Kanban stages such as Applied, Screening, Interview R1/R2, Technical Assessment, HR Round, Offer Sent and Hired. Rejected and Withdrawn are terminal states. Stage changes are audited.
  5. Schedule interviews: Store round type, interview panel, start/end time, time zone, meeting link or room and status. Status can become completed, rescheduled, cancelled or no-show.
  6. Evaluate candidate: Interviewers submit 1–5 ratings for technical ability, communication and culture fit, plus notes and a hire/hold/reject recommendation.
  7. Create offer: HR enters designation, department, compensation, currency, joining date, benefits and validity. State flow: draft → pending_approval → approved → sent → accepted.
  8. Onboard to HRMS: The bridge validates the candidate, creates a unique Employee record, migrates resume and offer letter to EmployeeDocument, marks the candidate hired, increments filled positions, updates offer status and logs the conversion.

Where is the candidate resume?

Recruitment → ATS Pipeline/Candidates → click candidate card → Candidate Drawer → Candidate Details → Resume Viewer → View/Preview/Download.

The candidate profile references the uploaded file through resumeUrl. After hiring, the same resume is available in the employee's HRMS document repository.

Talent Pool: meaning and flow

The Talent Pool is a reusable collection of candidates who may be suitable for current or future vacancies. It prevents good candidates from being lost when a job closes, a candidate is not selected for one role, or no matching job is currently open.

Candidate sourced/applied → profile and resume saved → skills/tags/search data maintained → candidate retained in pool → recruiter searches future opening → candidate linked/advanced to pipeline → interview/offer/hire or remains available
  • It can contain passive candidates, referrals, previous applicants, silver-medalist candidates and agency-sourced profiles.
  • Recruiters can search using skills, experience, location, notice period, source, previous stage and job compatibility.
  • Talent Pool is not the same as the active pipeline: the pipeline is tied to a particular job; the pool is reusable across jobs.
  • A candidate can move from the pool into a specific job's Applied or Screening stage without recreating the profile.

3. Expenses Module

The Expenses module controls employee business spending from receipt capture through two-level approval, reimbursement and accounting export.

Expense incurred → receipt upload/AI scan → multi-item claim → policy and budget validation → employee submission → manager approval → finance approval → reimbursement → ERP CSV export

Step-by-step flow

  1. Create claim: An employee bundles multiple items such as travel, hotel, meals or taxi. Each item stores category, date, merchant, description, amount, tax, currency, receipt and billable-to-client information.
  2. Scan receipt: Gemini Vision extracts merchant, date, amount, tax, currency, description and suggested category. The employee verifies the result; a fallback parser is available when AI is unavailable.
  3. Apply policy: Category rules check maximum claim limits, receipt thresholds, active status and GL code mapping.
  4. Check budget: The system calculates spent amount, pending amount, remaining amount and percentage used. Less than 80% is normal, 80–99% is warning and above 100% is a breach.
  5. Submit: Claim status changes from draft to submitted; a number such as EXP-2026-0012 and an activity record are created.
  6. Manager review: The line manager checks business purpose, receipt and departmental ownership. Approval gives manager_approved; rejection requires a reason. Self-approval is blocked.
  7. Finance review: Finance checks receipt, GL code, tax, budget and policy compliance. Approval gives approved.
  8. Reimburse and export: Finance records bank transfer, payroll, corporate card, cash or cheque, including transaction reference. The final state is reimbursed; CSV export supports ERP reconciliation.

4. Inventory Module

Inventory manages item masters, warehouses, procurement, receiving, stock valuation, traceability, equipment assets and supply-chain analytics.

Item/warehouse setup → Purchase Requisition → budget check → Purchase Order → Goods Receipt Note → immutable Stock Ledger → FIFO/WAC valuation → issue/transfer/asset assignment → analytics and accounting events

Step-by-step flow

  1. Configure master data: Define item SKU, category, UoM, barcode, reorder level and tracking type. Configure Warehouse → Zone → Bin and supplier terms.
  2. Create PR: A department requests material. The system checks budget through Expenses and prevents self-approval.
  3. Create PO: An approved PR becomes a supplier Purchase Order with prices, taxes, terms and versioned amendments.
  4. Receive with GRN: Warehouse staff record ordered, received, accepted and rejected quantities, plus batches or serial numbers and inspection results.
  5. Post ledger: Accepted receipt adds a positive StockLedger entry. Issues add negative entries. Corrections use compensating reversals rather than editing history.
  6. Protect stock: Available stock equals on-hand minus reserved stock. An issue exceeding available quantity is rejected before posting.
  7. Value stock: FIFO consumes the oldest cost layer first; Moving WAC recalculates the average cost after each receipt.
  8. Transfer and trace: Inter-warehouse movement follows dispatch → in_transit → receipt. Batch and serial lineage supports forward and backward traceability.
  9. Three-way match: The system compares Purchase Order, GRN and supplier invoice quantities and prices. Out-of-tolerance differences can block payment or create a debit note.
  10. Analyze: Forecasting, OCR, anomaly radar, ABC/XYZ matrix, aging, turnover, DSI and supplier OTIF reports support decisions.

5. HRMS Module

HRMS manages employees, organization hierarchy, attendance, shifts, leave, payroll, documents, performance and workforce intelligence.

User/Employee identity → organization and job profile → shift assignment → attendance → leave and accruals → payroll → payslip/payment → performance and career → offboarding

Employee and identity flow

The system keeps authentication identity in User and personnel data in Employee, linked through canonical identity resolution. Employee profiles include department, designation, branch, manager, statutory details, bank/payroll information, documents and job history.

Attendance and shifts

  1. HR configures morning, evening, night or custom shifts with grace and half-day rules.
  2. HR assigns a shift to the employee.
  3. The employee performs clock-in, break-in, break-out and clock-out.
  4. The system calculates worked minutes, lateness and attendance status.
  5. A missed punch can be submitted for manager regularization and automatic reconciliation.
  6. Weekend or holiday work may generate a compensatory-off credit.

Leave flow

  1. HR defines leave types, quotas, accrual, carry-forward and caps.
  2. Employee submits a request with dates and reason.
  3. The system previews sandwich-policy days where weekends or holidays fall between leave days.
  4. Manager approves or rejects. Approval debits the LeaveLedger; rejection restores any reserved balance.
  5. A monthly accrual process adds credits and records each transaction.

Payroll flow

Salary structure + attendance + approved leave + holidays + LOP → earnings and deductions → PayrollRun → Payslip → approval → paid
  • Salary structures define Basic, HRA, allowances, PF, PT, TDS and ESI.
  • Payroll reads attendance and approved leave to calculate loss-of-pay days.
  • Monthly payroll moves through draft → approved → paid.
  • PDF payslips contain attendance, earnings, deductions and net salary and are available in the employee self-service vault.

Performance and talent

Employees and managers use weighted OKRs, milestones, check-ins, 360-degree review cycles and the 3×3 performance-potential matrix. Attrition analysis uses attendance, leave, tenure and review trends to identify retention risk.

7. Complete Review Example

  1. Engineering creates a Software Engineer vacancy and publishes an AI-assisted job post.
  2. A candidate applies, enters the ATS pipeline, attends interviews and receives scorecards.
  3. HR approves and sends an offer; the candidate accepts.
  4. The HRMS bridge creates the employee and migrates the resume and offer letter.
  5. Inventory selects a Software Engineer kit and assigns a serial-numbered laptop.
  6. The employee punches attendance, applies for leave and is included in monthly payroll.
  7. The employee scans a client-meal receipt, submits an expense claim, receives manager and finance approval, and is reimbursed.
Final review statement: Nebula is not a set of isolated CRUD screens. It is an integrated, multi-tenant ERP workflow in which recruitment creates the workforce, HRMS governs the workforce, inventory equips the workforce, and expenses controls business spending.

8. Quick Viva Answers

  • What is the Talent Pool? A reusable database of potential candidates available for current or future vacancies, separate from a job-specific active pipeline.
  • Where is the resume? Recruitment → ATS Pipeline/Candidates → Candidate Card → Candidate Drawer → Candidate Details → Resume Viewer.
  • Why use an immutable inventory ledger? To preserve audit history and correct mistakes through linked reversals instead of silently changing past movements.
  • Why two expense approvals? The manager validates business purpose; Finance validates policy, accounting and payment.
  • What is the strongest integration? The Recruitment-to-HRMS bridge, because one accepted candidate becomes a complete employee without re-entering data.
